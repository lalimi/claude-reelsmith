# Examples

## Reference beat maps

Abstract shapes of the four reels the structures came from. Timings are typical for a 45–55 s cut, not measured from the originals. The source texts belong to their authors and are not reproduced.

**A — contrarian insight (marketing niche)**
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–3 s | contrarian hook: a national point of pride "isn't the cool part" |
| 3–15 s | three escalating everyday scenes of informal trust |
| 15–20 s | us vs them + named archetype |
| 20–27 s | the why: a historical cause, funny turns meaningful |
| 27–31 s | one-sentence insight re-reading the scenes |
| 31–40 s | bridge: how recommendations really travel in the niche |
| 40–45 s | bold statement against standard marketing advice |
| 45–50 s | CTA with a code word from scene 2; last line loops to it |

**B — series episode (family relocation vlog)**
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | series title + part 1 + stakes |
| 4–12 s | three options with a joke each → real reason → comic kicker |
| 12–18 s | first obstacle with counts (luggage, cars) |
| 18–26 s | small disaster + "only the first minutes of a long trip" |
| 26–33 s | expectation vs reality: braced for the worst, it went well |
| 33–38 s | plug: the creator's online business paid for it |
| 38–45 s | cliffhanger mid-scene at a border check |
| 45–50 s | teaser: next episode shows the house and the prices |

**C — humor hook + list (Reels-growth coach)**
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–6 s | stereotype + absurd escalation |
| 6–10 s | punchline pivot to the audience's pain (reels that flop) |
| 10–13 s | authority: N reels analysed, K factors |
| 13–48 s | K rules, ~3–4 s each, one demonstrated on camera as a meta-joke |
| 48–55 s | last factor = follow + DM keyword → free lesson |

**D — "the scenario of …" (money psychology)**
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–12 s | confession ×3: relationship → health → money, each last-minute |
| 12–18 s | generalise + hidden payoff: no crisis, no motivation |
| 18–26 s | coined name + cost: income always at survival level |
| 26–45 s | three steps; the third is a carrot-and-stick metaphor |
| 45–50 s | empowering close: a choice you can make today |

## Worked example: "The eternal-draft scenario" (structure D)

English adaptation of a Ukrainian reel written for a real product (a marketplace for digital products). The original Ukrainian script and its `reel.json` are in the repo's `examples/chernetka/`. Account: the founder's personal account. Voice-over over footage.

> I've spent three months rewriting my guide, and yesterday I changed the cover font for the <em>fourth time</em>. I won't sell until I hit <em>1,000 followers</em>, though two people already asked in my DMs where to buy. And when I dare, I'll charge <em>99</em> and give my best friend a discount. Because while it's a draft, nobody can say <em>no</em>. That's the eternal-draft scenario, and it's why Tanya from accounting has had a finished guide for <em>two years</em> and still lives paycheck to paycheck. The way out: announce a launch date in your stories before it's done, and set a price you're not ashamed to work for, not one you're not afraid to sell at. And remember, a draft is a <em>pie</em> you hide from guests while they fill up at the neighbor's. That's why I'm building [your product], a marketplace where you can list your guide and earn <em>from day one</em>. Living in drafts is a choice you can change <em>today</em>, so tell me in the comments how long yours has been waiting.

~175 words ≈ 58 s at a brisk Reels pace, right at the cap like the original. For 50 s, drop the discount clause and shorten step 2.

**Montage plan**

| Technique | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Doodle boil | 1–4 | "font #4" circled · "where to buy? ×2" + arrow · "99" struck through + "−20% for my bestie" · `guide_final_FINAL2.pdf` underlined |
| Orbit | 2 | "1,000 FOLLOWERS ·" rings the speaker's head on a cut-out selfie shot |
| Glass type | 5 | frosted title "the eternal-draft scenario" |
| Checklist | 7 | card "how to get out of drafts", two items tick in as spoken, sub-lines follow |
| Zoom-through cuts | into 2, 5, 9, 10 | outgoing shot rushes in; whips on the cuts into 3 and 8 |

The shipped cut also opens on a cover-story masthead, stacks a headline wall of draft file names under the glass title, runs beat 6 in black-and-white with a colour burst on "the way out", and punches in on accent words. Treat that as the upper limit, not the default.

**Notes**
- Localisation: "99" was 99 hryvnias (~$2.40): use your market's embarrassingly low price. A Ukrainian *kuma* (close family friend) became "best friend". Keep Tanya or swap in a name your audience reads as "a colleague we all know".
- Why it works: role-play confessions mock the speaker, so viewers recognise themselves without feeling attacked; the name lands at ~35% of runtime; the product enters at ~80% as the reason the creator builds it, not as an ad; the CTA asks for a number, the easiest comment to leave.
