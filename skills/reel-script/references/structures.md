# Structures

Four beat sequences distilled from viral reels. Beats are abstract; the short italic illustrations are invented examples, not quotes.

| Structure | Use when | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| A. Contrarian insight + named archetype | a broad "this is so us" truth that can be bridged into the niche | code word from a hook scene + loop back to the opening image |
| B. Series episode + cliffhanger | a real ongoing journey with stakes (launch, move, goal) | cliffhanger + teaser of the next episode |
| C. Humor hook → list of N factors | teaching N practical rules with authority | the last item is the CTA itself |
| D. "The scenario of …" (role-play confession) | a self-sabotaging habit the audience will recognise in themselves | empowering "it's a choice, make it today" (CTA optional) |
| E. "The experiment" (40 s) | you tried something real with stakes and have a result number | payoff number + one-line lesson + template on screen (save); code word = template name; last line loops to the first |
| F. "Reverse breakdown" (35–45 s) | a famous success everyone admires for the wrong reason | one quotable line + an open question that splits people; no code word (built for sends) |
| G. "POV scene" (20–28 s, loops) | a moment your audience lives through (first sale, first refund, a parent's reaction) | plug as a scene detail (a "new order" notification); final pose = opening pose |

---

## A. Contrarian insight + named archetype

1. **Contrarian hook**: what everyone is proud of or takes as obvious is "not the main thing; there's something truer". *Forget the famous landmarks.*
2. **Three hyper-specific "this is so us" scenes**, funny and escalating; the third is the most absurd. *The bag full of bags.*
3. **Us vs them** (foreigners, the system) embodied by a **named archetype** instead of "people". *We just ask Auntie Val.*
4. **The why**: the deeper cause (history, pain). The turn from funny to meaningful. *Because nobody else showed up.*
5. **One-sentence insight** that re-reads all three scenes.
6. **Bridge into the niche**: the same insight applied to the creator's topic.
7. **One bold statement** against the industry norm, with edge. *Your funnel can wait.*
8. **CTA with a code word** taken from a hook scene; the last line loops back to the opening image.

## B. Series episode + cliffhanger

1. **Title card**: series name + "Part N: <episode topic>" + the stakes in one line (why it matters, what could go wrong).
2. **Decision / setup**: quick-fire options with a one-line joke each, then the real reason, then a comic kicker reason.
3. **First obstacle with concrete numbers.** *Six boxes, one hatchback.*
4. **Escalation**: a funny small disaster + "and that was only the first X of Y". *And that was hour one.*
5. **Expectation vs reality** twist. *Braced for hell, got lucky.*
6. **Plug inside the story**: what paid for it or made it possible is the creator's product, said in passing, never at the end.
7. **Cliffhanger** at the tensest moment, cut mid-scene.
8. **Next-episode teaser** with a curiosity promise. *Next: what it all cost.*

## C. Humor hook → list of N factors

1. **Hook**: a universal stereotype + absurd comic escalation of it.
2. **Pivot to the audience's real pain** with a punchline that ties the stereotype to the niche.
3. **Authority**: "I analysed N … and found K factors". *I studied 300 launch pages.*
4. **List of K**: each item = rule + concrete example + (where possible) a live demonstration or meta-joke on camera.
5. **Value reminder**: one reel = one useful idea.
6. **The last item is the CTA**: follow → comment or DM a keyword → get a gift.

## D. "The scenario of …" — role-play confession → named pattern → steps

The creator speaks *as* the character in first person, then steps out and diagnoses.

1. **First-person confession**: three embarrassing, escalating examples of the same habit, ideally from different areas of life. *Fourth font change this week.*
2. **Generalise + hidden payoff**: what the habit protects the character from. *While it's unfinished, nobody judges.*
3. **Coin a name** ("This is the scenario of …") and show its **cost**, often through a named archetype.
4. **Three steps out**, each concrete; the third built on a metaphor.
5. **Empowering close**: "you don't have to live like this; it's a choice, and you can make it today". CTA optional; if used, a question that is easy to answer in one comment.

## E. "The experiment" — stakes, compressed stages (40 s)
Based on Jenny Hoyos' Shorts loop (hook → foreshadow ≤ 3 s → transition → but/therefore body → payoff → abrupt end).
1. **0–2 s** result teaser over the result screen ("I gave Claude Code my shop for 7 days…").
2. **2–5 s** the rule and the stakes ("one rule: I don't step in, even if it breaks everything").
3. **5–7 s** a transition line that pushes forward (not "so let's start").
4. **7–26 s** three compressed stages linked by *but / therefore*, each ending on a mini-rehook ("and on day three it got worse").
5. **26–32 s** payoff number on screen + a twist.
6. **32–37 s** the lesson in one sentence + the prompt/template on screen (save trigger).
7. **37–40 s** code word = the template's name, hard cut; the last line flows back into the first.

## F. "Reverse breakdown" — famous thing, real mechanism (35–45 s)
Kallaway's "context lean → interjection → snapback" used as a whole script.
1. **0–3 s** something famous everyone admires + 3–5 words on screen.
2. **3–5 s** "but that's the least interesting part".
3. **5–8 s** snapback to the real mechanism.
4. **8–28 s** three proof beats (screens, numbers) linked by *therefore / but*.
5. **28–36 s** practical value: "in your business it looks like this…".
6. **36–42 s** one bold quotable line + an open question. No code-word CTA.

## G. "POV scene" — escalating reactions (20–28 s, loops)
1. **0–1.5 s** on-screen "pov: you just sold your first guide", the creator plays the role.
2. **1.5–8 s** reaction 1.
3. **8–16 s** reactions 2 and 3, escalating; the third is the most absurd.
4. **16–22 s** the turn: one true line about the real pain.
5. **22–26 s** plug inside the scene (a phone notification), no spoken CTA.
6. **26–28 s** final pose = opening pose, so it loops.

## Device list (name these when analysing)

- **Hooks**: contrarian · stakes · absurd humor · confession · series title
- **Rule of three, escalating** (the third example is the funniest or most extreme)
- **Named character / archetype** instead of "people"
- **Coined term** for a pattern
- **Concrete numbers** (people, money, hours, counts)
- **Us vs them** contrast
- **Turn from funny to meaningful** (the "why")
- **One-sentence insight**
- **Show, don't tell** (demonstrate the mistake on camera)
- **Meta-joke** about the video itself
- **Expectation vs reality**
- **Plug inside the story** (not at the end)
- **Cliffhanger + teaser**
- **Endings**: code-word CTA from the hook · CTA that is the last tip · empowering close
